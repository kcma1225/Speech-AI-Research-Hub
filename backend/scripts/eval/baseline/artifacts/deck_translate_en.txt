## Dynamic Hotword Approach

![](diagrams/diagram-846f468b5b7f.svg){.hub-diagram fig-align="center" fig-alt="diagram" data-font="20.3"}

- Hotword list enters at the decoder, not the encoder
- Surveyed families: shallow fusion, TCPGen, contextual biasing

| Method | Where hotword information enters |
|---|---|
| Shallow fusion | Score added during decoding |
| TCPGen | Constrained generation inside the decoder |
| Contextual biasing | Bias states in the decoding graph |

::: {.takeaway}
Shallow fusion, TCPGen, and contextual biasing differ in where hotword information enters the model.
:::

::: {.notes}
The pipeline starts with speech input feeding into the Zipformer encoder, which is the streaming baseline already running on icefall. RNN-T decoding then applies hotword weighting: the user-provided hotword list, compiled into a trie, enters the decoder as a separate bias branch rather than through the acoustic encoder. The final output is the recognition result, where we check the errors on rare words. The table compares the three surveyed families by their injection point: shallow fusion adds a hotword score during decoding, TCPGen generates hotword subwords inside the decoder from a constrained pool, and contextual biasing builds the bias into the decoding graph. All three act on the decoder side, but the mechanism differs, and that difference is what the upcoming experiments will compare.
:::

## Idea Card

> Build the hotword list into a trie (prefix tree) and add score bonuses during RNN-T decoding

![](diagrams/diagram-c1a277950bdb.svg){.hub-diagram fig-align="center" fig-alt="diagram" data-font="20.9"}

::: {.takeaway}
Example: decoding N-a-v-i-d stays on the trie, so the rare name wins the bonus and is recognized correctly.
:::

::: {.notes}
This idea is the presenter's own view rather than a method reported in the reviewed papers. The hotword list is compiled into a trie (prefix tree), which lets the decoder cheaply check at every step whether the current hypothesis matches a prefix of any hotword. During RNN-T decoding, paths that stay on the trie receive a score bonus, biasing the output toward the intended keywords. Because the bonus only applies on prefix matches, common words keep their normal scores, so the approach targets rare or domain-specific terms without disturbing everyday recognition. In the illustration, the names Navid and Nadia share the prefix Na; when the decoder emits N-a-v-i-d it stays on the trie, and the completed path earns the bonus, so the rare name is recognized correctly.
:::
