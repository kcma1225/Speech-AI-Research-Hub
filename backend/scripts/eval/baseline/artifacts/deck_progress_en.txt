::::

::: {.takeaway}
Make user-provided hotwords recognizable in Zipformer + RNN-T streaming ASR — without retraining the whole model.
:::

::: {.notes}
End-to-end ASR output is biased toward its training vocabulary, so proper nouns, personal names, and internal jargon are poorly recognized — and these words often appear only at inference time (literature review note, 2026-10-24; nakagome2025wctc; wang2023contextual). This motivates our research question: in Zipformer + RNN-T streaming ASR, can user-provided hotwords such as names and course terms be made easier to recognize without retraining the whole model (research brief; survey note, 2026-10-24)? The diagram shows this failure path: the spoken hotword enters the streaming recognizer, but decoding stays biased toward the training vocabulary, so the word comes out dropped or misspelled. Our own data makes the problem concrete. First, when we built a 320-term hotword list from course recordings, CLAS attention diluted across the list and rare-word recall dropped (weekly meeting notes, 2026-10-20). Second, streaming makes biasing harder: the lookahead is only 320 ms, so bias scores easily false-trigger and insert spurious hotwords (weekly meeting notes, 2026-10-20). Next, I will present candidate biasing routes organized by injection position and retraining cost.
:::

## Idea or Architecture

- Taxonomy: injection position × retraining cost
- Four routes: shallow fusion, CLAS, adapters, adaptive biasing

![](diagrams/diagram-0f311ae53b57.svg){.hub-diagram fig-align="center" fig-alt="diagram" data-font="17.1"}

::: {.takeaway}
Compare routes by injection position and retraining cost
:::

::: {.notes}
Our organizing idea is a taxonomy of dynamic hotword biasing for streaming RNN-T, with two axes: injection position (encoder, decoder, search stage) and whether retraining is required (paper outline draft, 2026-10-24). The diagram marks the three candidate injection points on the Zipformer + RNN-T pipeline. Four routes: shallow fusion is training-free but may false-trigger (zhao2019shallow); CLAS injects hotword embeddings into the decoder and needs training (pundak2018deep); contextual adapters keep the backbone frozen and train only a small adapter (sathyendra2022contextual); adaptive biasing toggles biasing by context (xu2023adaptive). Our notes add stakes: with 320 hotwords, CLAS attention dilutes and rare-word recall drops, and the 320 ms lookahead causes false triggers (weekly meeting notes 2026-10-20).
:::
